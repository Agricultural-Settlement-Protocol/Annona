# STRATEGIC BRIEF — KMP Alternative Entities & Annona Revenue Model

> Companion strategic document to the v4.0 change set (`00`–`04`). Answers two questions the core change docs don't cover: (1) if the KMP role is not filled by a government-mandated village cooperative, who else could occupy that slot in the protocol, and (2) how Annona actually earns money given it is, by design, not a payment rail. All external facts below were verified in July 2026; re-verify again before any pitch, as funding programs and figures move fast.

---

## PART A — Entities that could substitute for KMP

### A.1 The architectural premise (why this is even possible)

In the Annona contract, the cooperative is not a hard-coded institution — it is one `Address` bound to the `coop` role: pre-funds cash, drafts the agreement, accepts supply, records delivery, calls `settle`. Nothing in the settlement math, the double-confirmation gates, or the reputation system requires that address to belong to a KDMP-registered cooperative specifically. **Swapping who plays "KMP" is a seeding/onboarding decision, not a contract change** — the same "per-record Address, not a global singleton" property already documented for the supplier and financier roles in `02-CHANGES-SMART-CONTRACT.md` §A. This is worth stating explicitly in the pitch as an architecture strength: Annona is an offtake-settlement _protocol_, and the identity of the offtaker is a parameter, not an assumption.

**The one invariant that must survive regardless of who fills the role:** HPP stays the settlement floor (never negotiated down by whoever occupies the offtaker seat), the three-way split stays transparent on-chain, and under-tolerance delivery stays a human-reviewed flag, never an automatic penalty against the farmer. If the substitute entity is a profit-seeking private party, these invariants are _more_ important, not less — they are what stops the "offtaker" role from quietly becoming a new tengkulak with better UX.

### A.3 One cautionary data point that validates Annona's thesis rather than undermines it

**TaniFund** (the P2P lending arm of TaniHub Group, launched 2017) ran a private-sector version of "finance farmers against expected harvest" without a tamper-evident verification layer. Result, per OJK's own findings: only **36% of 90-day loans were repaid**, OJK sanctioned the platform for failing to resolve defaults, and **TaniFund's operating license was formally revoked in May 2024**. This is a directly relevant, validated cautionary case: private capital _is_ willing to fund exactly this loop, but **without independently verifiable proof of the underlying transaction, default rates become unmanageable** — which is precisely the gap Annona's tamper-evident settlement record is built to close. Cite this case carefully as a _cautionary precedent_, not as a currently-operating comparable — TaniFund is defunct.

## PART B — Annona revenue model

### B.1 The hard constraint to state up front, honestly

Annona does **not**, and legally **cannot**, take a cut by intercepting the money that flows through settlement. Two independent reasons: (1) crypto-as-payment is illegal in Indonesia under the Currency Law, so no version of Annona — testnet or eventual mainnet — can itself function as, or skim from, a live rupiah payment rail; (2) even the on-chain dIDR movements in the demo are explicitly non-production (testnet-only, "never claim testnet tokens are live rupiah"). **Monetization has to sit in a different layer than the payment flow itself** — software/service, data, and facilitation, not the transfer.

### B.3 The one sentence to use in the pitch

**"Annona monetizes the software, data, and facilitation layers built on top of a transaction record — never the payment itself."** This framing is both more legally defensible (Annona never claims to be, or profit from being, a payment rail) and more credible to a finance-literate judge, because it mirrors monetization patterns that are already proven in Web3 infrastructure (block explorers, oracle networks, on-chain credit data) rather than a promise to eventually intercept money once regulation permits — the latter is a weaker, more speculative claim.
